Field note

How one private school stopped running the office in pieces.

We do not name the campus here. The account is a private school that helped shape AnnaSIS and now uses the operating system in ordinary time — not a brochure season.

The school did not need another portal. It needed the office, Parents, Students, and Faculty to share one record.

The condition

Like many private schools, academic records, tuition, activity fees, and seasonal registration had grown into separate tools. Parents received more than one notice for a single Student. Faculty and the front desk did not share a single list for who was cleared to play. The office reconciled names before QuickBooks could close.

What changed

The campus put AnnaSIS under the school’s own site. Application, tuition, incidental fees, events, and the store sit on the household. Faculty, Parents, and Students see grades on the same record. Notices go out by email and SMS without a required app. QuickBooks remains the books of record; the operating system posts the work of the office rather than replacing the accountant.

Athletics and other multi-step processes run as workflows the school configures — serial or parallel approvals — instead of a second forms vendor. Used uniforms can move through a school-facilitated exchange beside the store.

What the office reports

Staff describe fewer exports for a single family, a calmer path for Parents, and less time spent resetting logins. Parents, Students, and Faculty meet a portal that matches what the office already knows. Operating effort fell because work stopped being copied between systems — not because the school added a coordinator to manage another vendor.

How this is used

The campus also helps a small user group of peer schools name the next features that remove their own pain. That is the point of a school operating system written with a school: the roadmap is not a district backlog.

Figures that belong in a formal write-up are shared in conversation, with the school’s permission. If you want the one-page outline or twenty minutes on what is similar at your campus, ask.
