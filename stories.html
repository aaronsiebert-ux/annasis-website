Real world

How private schools stop running the office in pieces.

A system designed in close connection with a private school — shaped around real campus challenges and the features the office actually needed.

The school did not need another portal. It needed the office, Parents, Students, and Faculty to share one system.

The condition

Like many private schools, academic records, tuition, activity fees, events, athletics forms, and store purchases had grown into separate tools — the jobs a typical SIS rarely covers together. Accounting was separate. Parents received more than one notice for a single Student. Faculty and the front desk did not share a single list for who was cleared to play. The office reconciled names before QuickBooks could close. The school was challenged to meet budget needs with several systems and resource-intensive processes.

What changed

The household runs on one system across the foundation modules: Admissions, SIS, Gradebook, tuition & billing, health & medical records, school store, communication, event registration, donations & fundraising, QuickBooks accounting, uniform exchange, and athletics eligibility & approvals. Faculty, Parents, and Students see grades on the same system. Notices go out by email and SMS without a required app. Easy accounting with a single system with ACH, Stripe, and QuickBooks integrations. The school operating system posts the work of the office rather than replacing the accountant.

Where most SIS platforms fall short — and where ANNASIS is built to be different — is putting event registration, school ecommerce, athletics and sports, forms and approval workflows, the school store, and uniform exchange in the same system. Some peers cover one or two of those jobs; few cover all of them without a bolt-on stack. Athletics and other multi-step processes run as workflows the school configures — serial or parallel approvals — instead of a second forms vendor. Used uniforms move through a school-facilitated exchange beside the store. No more events planned in ways that are unfriendly to Parents and create extra work for staff.

What the office reports

Leadership, faculty, and staff describe a reduction in complexity and time, a calmer path for Parents, and less time spent on essential processes without a solution. Parents, Students, and Faculty meet a portal that matches what the office already knows. Operating effort and cost fell because work stopped being copied between departments and systems — not because the school added a coordinator to manage another vendor. Parents and Students received an improved campus experience.

How this is used

As a foundation for success, this model is being rolled out to a group of peer schools that will participate in improving the future of the ANNASIS operating system — and thus their own school experience. That is the point of a school operating system written with private schools: the roadmap is not a public-school backlog. Education matters, and so do the schools that provide it.
