Designed with and for our customers

We adapted the SIS to the school’s vision — together.

This is not a catalog install. ANNASIS was shaped in close partnership with a private school: we listened to the office, learned what “success” meant for that campus, and tailored the platform so the SIS became the school operating system they actually wanted to run.

Designed with and for our customers. Partnership first — then the product that matches the vision.

The partnership

Leadership and the office had a clear picture of how Parents, Students, and Faculty should experience the school — one household path, fewer tools, and processes that matched how the campus already worked. Off-the-shelf SIS options stopped short of that vision. We treated the engagement as a design partnership: sit with the people who run admissions, tuition, events, athletics, and the store; map the gaps; and adapt ANNASIS module by module until the system reflected their school, not a generic district template.

The condition we started from

Like many private schools, academic records, tuition, activity fees, events, athletics forms, and store purchases had grown into separate tools — the jobs a typical SIS rarely covers together. Accounting was separate. Parents received more than one notice for a single Student. Faculty and the front desk did not share a single list for who was cleared to play. The office reconciled names before QuickBooks could close. The school was challenged to meet budget needs with several systems and resource-intensive processes.

What we built together

The household runs on one system across the foundation: Core SIS (family record, Admissions, Attendance, schedules, Gradebook, report cards, transcripts, the Parent portal and communication, and health records) plus Power-ups — tuition, billing & fundraising, school store, event registration, athletics eligibility & approvals, uniform exchange, and QuickBooks accounting. Faculty, Parents, and Students see grades on the same system. Notices go out by email and SMS without a required app. Easy accounting with a single system with ACH, Stripe, and QuickBooks integrations. The school operating system posts the work of the office rather than replacing the accountant.

Where most SIS platforms fall short — and where partnership made the difference — is putting event registration, school ecommerce, athletics and sports, forms and approval workflows, the school store, and uniform exchange in the same system, configured to this campus’s vision. Athletics and other multi-step processes run as workflows the school configures — serial or parallel approvals — instead of a second forms vendor. Used uniforms move through a school-facilitated exchange beside the store. Events are planned in ways that are friendly to Parents and lighter on staff.

What the office reports

Leadership, faculty, and staff describe a reduction in complexity and time, a calmer path for Parents, and less time spent on essential processes without a solution. Parents, Students, and Faculty meet a portal that matches what the office already knows. Operating effort and cost fell because work stopped being copied between departments and systems — not because the school added a coordinator to manage another vendor. Parents and Students received an improved campus experience.

How this partnership continues

As a foundation for success, this model is being rolled out with peer schools that participate in improving the future of the ANNASIS operating system — and thus their own school experience. That is the point of software designed with and for our customers: the roadmap is shaped by the offices that run it. Education matters, and so do the schools that provide it.
